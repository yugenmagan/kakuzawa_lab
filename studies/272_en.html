Sometimes, Other Manga

I never neglect my efforts to evangelize for Elfen Lied, but hardly anyone seems to appreciate just how interesting it is. Not many people have bought the collected volumes after I introduced the series to them. Still, my friends are quite happy to read it, and whenever a new volume comes out, they come over asking, “Let me read it.”

The other day, one of those friends told me, “If you like that one, give these a try,” and recommended the following manga.

(I have italicized both plot summaries merely to distinguish them from the rest of the text; they are not quotations from the works.)

Alien 9 (Hitoshi Tomizawa; Akita Shoten)

The school’s “alien countermeasures team” has the grueling task of capturing aliens that, for some reason, keep falling from the sky. Of all people, Yuri, an elementary school girl who is more frightened than anyone else, gets saddled with the job and faces a terrible ordeal…

I had actually read this one already. Leaving aside the creator’s particular tastes, it is extremely well made both in storytelling and in the expressive possibilities of manga. Young girls fighting with something resembling vectors and undergoing horrible experiences may indeed be points it shares with Elfen Lied. Another common thread is the evolution of species. As suggested by the book depicted in the library in the background, its roots seem to lie in The Holon Revolution. Looking to outer space for the trigger of evolution is decidedly New Age.

Note: It has none of the emotional warmth of Okamoto’s work. If this description makes you want to read it, be warned: it is depressing.

Gunslinger Girl (Yu Aida; MediaWorks)

An agency founded by the government ostensibly to protect people with disabilities is in fact a special organization that gives orphaned girls artificial bodies, brainwashes them with drugs, and makes them carry out assignments such as assassinations…

Innocent girls, guns, and killing. It is less blatant than the work above, but this hardly seems in particularly good taste either. I am not quite sure why this one was recommended to me. Perhaps it is because cute girls kill people left and right, or because the relationship between the heroine and the man assigned to her resembles that between Nana and Kurama.

Compared with the other title, this seems the most understated, but drugging children to brainwash them is pretty gruesome. Despite its intelligence-agency setting, the emphasis seems to be less on missions than on the relationships between the girls and their male handlers. In fact, it could be called a fairly pure moe manga (though apparently only one volume has been released so far, so it is hard to say).

Are Nikita and Léon the inspirations? It feels as though someone mixed the two together, added an enormous helping of “moe,” and diluted the result.

I do not dislike either work. More than learning anything about these manga, though, I now have a much clearer idea of how Elfen Lied must look to the friend who recommended them to me (laughs).

March 5, 2003
