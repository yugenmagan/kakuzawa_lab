Elfen Lied and the Bible

Introduction

As for the author, Dai, see the “Dai” page at Kakuzawa Laboratory (provisional name)… Here I only want to offer conjectures. The content is not very different from “Dai”…

It began when the Bible came up in a chat. I had taken a biblical studies class, so I became curious about the relationship between Elfen Lied and the Bible and looked into it.

These are purely conjectures, not an attempt to convert anyone or encourage Bible reading…

Here is a brief account of what I found.

Adam and Eve

The Old Testament says that Adam and Eve, expelled from Paradise, had two sons: Cain, the elder, and Abel, the younger.

Cain grew jealous of Abel, who continued to enjoy God's favor, and eventually killed him. Cain was marked as a sinner and banished eastward.

Conjecture. Suppose the director is Adam, Lucy's mother is Eve, Lucy is Cain, and Lucy's younger brother is Abel. Lucy kills her brother, and the director mentions Adam and Eve in the story. This suggests a fairly deep biblical connection. Though Lucy is a girl…

The Ark (Ark of the Covenant)

The Ark is a mysterious chest used to hold the stone tablets on which Moses inscribed the Ten Commandments.

According to the Old Testament, it emits light and poisonous clouds, inflicts burns, and kills those who touch or open it (apparently Jews are allowed to touch it). It is also said that whoever takes the Ark into battle cannot lose.

It was supposedly carried to Ethiopia by Menelik, King Solomon's son, and has not been found to this day.

Conjecture. The Ark and the vectors seem to share many characteristics.

There is a Hollywood Indiana Jones film, Raiders of the Lost Ark, in which Indy, acting on American orders, tries to obtain the Ark before the Nazis do.

A pattern emerges: the Nazis' ambition = the director's ambition; the Ark = the vectors; Elfen Lied and Lebensborn = German words.

In other words, the Nazis sought the Ark to win the war (because its bearer supposedly never loses a battle), just as the director sought the vectors (Lucy) to become the god of a new world. One could even say that the Ark = Lucy.

There may also be a profound link between the Ark's journey to Ethiopia and the discovery there of Lucy, the woman described as a common ancestor of modern humanity, the “African Eve” (as noted at Kakuzawa Laboratory [provisional name]).

The Dead Sea Scrolls

The Dead Sea Scrolls were found in caves near the Dead Sea, close to Israel (the lowest exposed body of water on Earth). They are said to represent one of four kinds of ancient source texts that existed before the Jewish compilation of the Old Testament.

Only some have been discovered, however, and many mysteries remain.

Conjecture. The underground lake where Anna lived and the Dead Sea have something in common: one is deep beneath the earth, while the other occupies the lowest point on its surface.

Some theories also claim that the Scrolls conceal an elaborate code predicting events in the future. This may be closely related to Anna's prophetic powers.

The Seventy-Two Demons

The seventy-two demons are said here to be beings summoned from the underworld by King Solomon and mentioned in the New Testament's Book of Revelation.

They are assigned numbers from 1 to 72 in the order in which they appear in scripture. Summoned demons do more than attack enemies: they devise strategies, provide intelligence, and teach alchemy.

Nor do they all look demonic; some resemble humans or animals.

Conjecture. I think the Diclonius numbers 3, 7, 28 and 35 have much in common with demons bearing the same numbers. Here is how.

Demon No. 3 — Vassago

Reveals new knowledge or women's secrets to the summoner.

When Diclonius No. 3 infected Kurama with the virus, he acquired new knowledge (his daughter's infection?) and a woman's secret (his wife was carrying a Diclonius daughter? Or again the daughter's infection?). A bit of a stretch…

Demon No. 7 — Amon

He can instill hostility between two parties or mediate to end a conflict. He has a stern character and knows true love. Moreover, this account says that the Gospel of Matthew mentions Mammon, a demon derived from Amon who instills a craving for money in humans. He is therefore portrayed as a symbol of greed, one of the seven deadly sins.

Nana can be a troublemaker, but she also eases tensions; she is stern and becomes furious at inhumane acts (as during the Man in the Hat's attack). I think she also has true love for Kurama (although that is another stretch…). Unlike Mammon, Nana is indifferent to money, but very greedy when it comes to food.

Demon No. 28 — Berith

A terrifying marquis of Hell, appearing in a golden crown and crimson armor astride a red horse. He possesses tremendous military strength and advanced alchemical knowledge, but is also Hell's greatest liar.

This sounds more like the Man in the Hat than the Diclonius turned into a radar. Hidden under a black hat and coat, he terrorized people with spiked balls…

Demon No. 35 — Marchosias

A mighty marquis of Hell who appears as a winged, fire-breathing wolf, yet is loyal and fiercely hates lies and improper conduct.

That is practically Mariko herself. She cut Ms. Saito in half for lying while pretending to be her mother…

A provisional conclusion

There is certainly a fair amount of personal interpretation here, but I think Okamoto-sensei drew at least some inspiration from the Bible when planning Elfen Lied.

Of course my findings are subjective and by no means necessarily correct. This is simply reading deeply into the work.

Still, Okamoto-sensei really is thoughtful…

I believe, however, that the work's greatest theme is discrimination.

Lucy and the director were angered by human discrimination against Diclonius (I think the work has strong links to Nazi persecution and mass murder of Jews). Yet the director was not actually a Diclonius, which is why Lucy never trusted the Kakuzawa family…

But the two did not reach the same destination. The director, whose outlook might be called a persecution complex, and Lucy, who ultimately gave up revenge, made different choices. I think the author's message lies in that difference. That message is love.

Though personally, the eroticism left a stronger impression on me than violence or love… (Along with all those things to nitpick.)

Additional notes

Lilith and Belial

Lilith is a famous female demon said to have been Adam's first wife. She later parted from him and bore many demons with fallen angels and others.

Belial is one of the seventy-two demons, numbered 68. He is said to rank just below Lucifer, while also being the most licentious and obscene fallen angel. His magical power is immense, and he is handsome and majestic, though his soul is utterly hideous.

I had hoped that a third part might introduce another wife of the director, modeled on Lilith, or Diclonius No. 68, stronger than Mariko (entirely my personal speculation, of course…). But now it is the final chapter… What a shame. Still, perhaps there will be a side story…!!
